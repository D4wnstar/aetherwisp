The **absolute threshold** in psychophysics is the minimum intensity that a stimulus must have in order to be perceived. Any stimulus of intensity weaker than the absolute threshold will not be perceived.
### Light threshold
Hecht, Shlaer and Pirenne (1942) first measured the absolute threshold of [[Electromagnetic radiation|light]]. They place an observer in a dark room, fully adapted to the dark. They then emitted very brief flashes of light (about 1 ms of exposure) with variable quantities of around 24 to 220 [[photon|photons]] in each flash. They then asked the observer if they saw a flash of light or not. They found that a flash is perceived on average when 10 photoreceptors receive one photon.
