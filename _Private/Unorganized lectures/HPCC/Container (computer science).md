A **container**, in computer science, is a lightweight isolated environment for applications. It is similar in concept to a [[virtual machine]], with the main difference being that unlike a VM, a container does not maintain a guest [[operating system|OS]] and instead shares the kernel of the host OS. This makes containers much faster to start up and subject to much less overhead, at the cost of being unable to have an entire separate OS running in your machine. They are thus less secure in terms of isolation. They are commonplace in cloud computing because they make packaging apps and all their dependencies much more convenient, thus avoiding dependency hell on remote servers.

On Linux, a container is a regular [[Process (computer science)|process]] with specific restrictions: managing the namespace and the control groups. The namespace restricts what the container can see (in particular, it can only see itself), while control groups (cgroups) limit what resources the container can use (CPU, I/O, memory, etc.).
