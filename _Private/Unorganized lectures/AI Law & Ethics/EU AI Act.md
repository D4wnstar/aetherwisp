The **EU AI Act** (formally: **Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 laying down Harmonised Rules on Artificial Intelligence (2024)**) is a regulation devised by the European Union to govern the use of artificial intelligence systems. The AI Act encompasses the use of all AI systems, depending on their use case and their potential for harm.

Binding text available on [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng).

The AI Act's entrance into force is split in four steps: prohibitions were enforced in February 2025; general-purpose AI rules were enforce in August 2025; rules on all other AI systems (excluding high-risk AI) are to be enforced in August 2026; rules on high-risk AI are to be enforced in August 2027.

Briefly, the act applies to (Art. 2):
- Anyone placing an AI system into the market or into service in the EU, regardless of where the provider is established.
- Any deployer of an AI system established in the EU.
- Any deployer of an AI system, established in or out of the EU, where the output of the system is used in the EU.
- Importers and exporters of AI systems.
- Product manufacturers placing on the market an AI system alongside their product and under their name or trademark.
- Authorized representative of providers not established in the EU.
- Affected persons located in the EU.

The AI Act explicitly does not apply to, among others (Art. 2):
- Systems created solely for the purpose of scientific research and development.
- Personal, non-professional projects by natural persons.
- Systems intended for use in national security, defense and military.

The AI Act defines "AI system" as (Art. 3 (1)):
> \[A\] machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments

The prohibitions (Art. 5) broadly consist of:
- Usage of AI for subliminal and/or subversive manipulation of behavior that leads to significant harm to one or more persons.
- Usage of AI for exploitation of vulnerabilities (age, disability, specific social or economic situation) to manipulate behavior in a way that causes significant harm to one or more persons.
- Usage of AI for social scoring that leads to (a) unfair treatment of natural persons in contexts unrelated to the data used for the scoring, or (b) unfair treatment that is unjustified or disproportionate to the gravity of the social behavior.
- Usage of AI for determining risk of criminal offence based solely on the person's profile or personality traits.
- Usage of AI to create or expand facial recognition databases through untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage.[^1]
- Usage of AI to infer emotions of natural persons in workplaces or educational institutions.
- Usage of biometric categorization systems to deduce sensitive information (as per GDPR, so political opinions, sexual orientations...) from biometric data of natural persons.
- Usage of real-time remote biometric identification systems in publicly accessible spaces for the purpose of law enforcement, unless the system is used for: (a) searching victims of abduction, human trafficking or missing persons; (b) prevention of immediate substantial threats to physical safety or life of natural persons or a present or foreseeable terrorist attack; or (c) localization or identification of an alleged criminal for the sole purpose of conducting an investigation or executing penalty for offences defined in Annex II.

"High-risk AI" is defines as follows:
> Irrespective of whether an AI system is placed on the market or put into service independently of the products referred to in points (a) and (b), that AI system shall be considered to be high-risk where both of the following conditions are fulfilled:
> 	(a) the AI system is intended to be used as a safety component of a product, or the AI system is itself a product, covered by the Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I;
> 	(b)  the product whose safety component pursuant to point (a) is the AI system, or the AI system itself as a product, is required to undergo a third-party conformity assessment, with a view to the placing on the market or the putting into service of that product pursuant to the Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I.

[^1]: This particular prohibition is somewhat directly derived from the ClearView AI scandal.
