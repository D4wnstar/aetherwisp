The **central limit theorem** states that the [[probability distribution]] of a rescaled [[Arithmetic mean|sample mean]] converges, in some conditions, to a [[Gaussian distribution|normal distribution]]. The theorem has gone through numerous iterations and variations throughout the history of statistics and probability theory and as such, there exists several different variations.

> [!info] Classical central limit theorem
> Consider a sample of $n$ [[iid]] [[Random variable|random variables]] $X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}$ with [[expected value]] $\mu$ and finite [[variance]] $\sigma ^{2}$. Its sample mean is $\bar{X}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}$. As $n\to \infty$, the standardized random variables
> $$\frac{\bar{X}_{n}-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{ n }}$$
> [[convergence in distribution|converge in distribution]] to a normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$:
> $$\frac{\bar{X}_{n}-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{ n }}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1)$$
