In statistics, a **random sample** or just **sample** can refer to one of two definitions:
1. A [[set]] of [[Random variable|random variables]], $\{ X_{1},\ldots,X_{N} \}$;
2. A set of realizations from one or more random variables, $\{ x_{1},\ldots,x_{N} \}$.

Definition 1 is more rigorous and generally what is intended when talking about probability theory and statistical modeling. Definition 2 is the more intuitive one, as it considers a sample to simply be a set of numbers, and is more common in practical branches like data analysis and [[machine learning]]. Both definitions are in use and care should be taken when discussing samples, as it's often unclear which is being used.

A sample is a finite subset of the total **population**; for instance, measurements ran on one-hundred people constitute a sample of the total human population.

The fundamental principle of statistics is that conclusions drawn from a sample are approximately representative of the properties of the total population.
