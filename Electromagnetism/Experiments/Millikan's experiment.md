**Millikan's experiment** is intended to measure the [[elementary charge]], the [[electric charge]] of the [[electron]].

The experiment employs a cloud of sprayed liquid. The spray will contain fine droplets of the liquid of more or less constant size. Commonly, oils are used for the spray since they are cheap and easy to obtain, non-volatile and make spherical droplets. The utility of this is that the droplets can brush each other in the cloud and in so doing, exchange electric charges; electrons to be specific.

Now, droplets have [[mass]], so they tend to fall due to [[gravity]]. They also interact with air [[Molecule|molecules]], so they are kept up due to fluid dynamics. We can summarize all of this in a single force equation:
$$F=- \frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\rho _\text{spray}g+ \frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\rho _\text{air}g+6\pi \mu rv$$
where $\rho _\text{spray}$ and $\rho _\text{air}$ are the mass densities of the spray cloud and air, $g$ is the [[gravitational acceleration]], $r$ is the radius of the droplets, $\mu$ is the drag coefficient (???) and $v$ is the falling velocity (???). This is under the assumption of spherical droplets of constant size. At equilibrium, the net force must be null, which yields:
$$\frac{4}{3}\pi gr^{2}(\rho _\text{spray}-\rho _\text{air})=6\pi \mu v_{t}$$
where $v_{t}$ is the terminal falling velocity at equilibrium. The radius then must be
$$r=\sqrt{ \frac{9\pi \mu v_{t}}{2\pi g(\rho _\text{spray}-\rho _\text{air})} }$$
These droplets are tiny, but they are visible at the right angle due to [[Electromagnetic radiation|light]] reflection and refraction.

We can have the cloud fall through a sufficiently large parallel plate [[capacitor]]. This capacitor has an [[electric field]] of $E=V/d$. If the droplets are charged, then they will be subject to a [[Lorentz force]] equal to
$$F=Q\frac{V}{d}\quad\Rightarrow \quad Q=\frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}g(\rho _\text{spray}-\rho _\text{air})d}{V}$$
